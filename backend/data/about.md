## Research background

Shashi's completed master's research is in classical wireless communication. His
thesis, **"Generalization of Downlink and Uplink NOMA Framework for Future Generation
Technology,"** studied multi-user power-domain NOMA systems. He developed analytical
models and MATLAB Monte Carlo simulations to evaluate bit-error rate and throughput
under flat Rayleigh fading, interference, power allocation, distance-based user
ordering, successive-interference-cancellation depth, SIC errors, and oversampling.
The work covered two-to-four-user cases as well as a generalized N-user framework and
included NOMA-versus-OMA throughput comparisons.

A manuscript based on part of the thesis, **"Analytical BER Study with Hardware
Validation for N-User NOMA,"** is under internal faculty review before planned
submission to an IEEE Transactions journal. It has not yet entered IEEE peer review.

## Research direction

Shashi's primary prospective PhD interest is quantum communication and quantum
information networking. He is building his foundation through open textbooks,
research papers, courses, standards material, and educational lectures. Topics he is
currently studying include quantum information representation, mathematical and
physical models of quantum channels, quantum key distribution and quantum
cryptography, quantum repeaters and networks, quantum error correction, and hybrid
security architectures that combine QKD with post-quantum cryptography.

These are active learning interests, not claims of completed quantum research. His
broader interests span 6G and beyond wireless systems, optical communication,
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), non-terrestrial and satellite networks,
multiple access including NOMA, and physical-layer signal processing. He is especially
interested in research that connects rigorous mathematical modelling with future
communication systems.
